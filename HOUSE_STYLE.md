# HOUSE STYLE — the Casually Explained gag grammar (v1)

Everything we ship must obey this file. It is the 5%: the difference
between "decent animation" and "ours". Derived from gag_proof_v2 and
windows 01–03, not from theory.

## 1. Cast grammar

- The stick-figure host is the viewer's surrogate. He is NEVER off-screen
  during a reaction beat — if the camera punches, he scurries into frame
  first (gagA, w2a, w2c proved this three times).
- Guests enter from the NEAREST edge and never cross another actor
  (`walkerX` for left marks, `walkerXR` for right marks).
- Carried things ride overhead (y≈470) or at hand height beside the
  carrier — never through a face, never balanced on a head.
- Entrances land 100–300ms BEFORE their spoken word; payoffs land ON it.
- Idle is forbidden: breathing, blinks, weight shifts, prop fidgets.
  A frozen figure reads as a bug, not a pause.

## 2. Prop grammar (zero words)

- No on-screen text, ever. Numbers, labels, and dialogue live in the VO.
- Props are verbs, not nouns: envelopes SEAL, buttons get SLAMMED 3x,
  money RAINS, trays get CARRIED OFF, bellies DRAIN, knobs WHIP.
- Every prop has a full life: enter → act → payoff → exit-or-settle.
  Nothing pops in without anticipation (shrink to 90% first) and nothing
  vanishes without shrinking, flying off, or being confiscated.
- Confiscated/grabbed props shrink into the grab.

## 3. Camera grammar (our editing signature)

- Vocabulary only: WIDE (establish, 1.25x), PUNCH (payoff, 1.5–1.65x),
  MACRO (object gag, 1.6x+), TAG (closer, ends the window). No drifts,
  no slow zooms — the camera CUTS or PUNCHES, it never wanders.
- Hard cuts on sentence/window boundaries. Inside a window, punch-ins
  glide fast (settle <0.5s) and always keep the reactor in frame.
- Camera answers the joke: punch the OBJECT on the payoff word, punch
  the FACE on the reaction word. Never the reverse.
- Restraint: at most one punch per skit, two per window. More reads as panic.

## 4. Cut grammar

- Windows end on sentence boundaries (see `scripts/GAG_WINDOWS.md`).
- A window's last frame must be a tableau that survives freezing:
  cast settled, props at rest, no mid-stride limbs, no half-popped props.
- Callbacks beat novelty: the pendulum, the couch, the button return
  across windows. Repetition with escalation IS the show's memory.

## 5. Motion grammar

- One easing palette only (`enter`, `balanced`, `overshoot` — see
  MOTION_RESEARCH.md). `easeIO` is banned for payoffs; overshoot is
  mandatory on grabs, slams, pops, and arrivals.
- Squash on impact, stretch on launch. Volume is preserved, comedy isn't.
- Secondary motion lags one beat: hair, held props, bellies arrive late.
- Fast moves get smears; teleporting gets a poof. Nothing just appears.
